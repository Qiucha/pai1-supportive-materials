Six weeks of probability and artificial intelligence

Probability for AI

Try a probability model. Fit its parameters. Change a network's prediction. Then decide what evidence you need to judge a system.

This learning material is for learners with or without course access. Each lesson explains the ideas it relies on without requiring access to the Probability for AI course.

Unofficial. This material is not an official Stanford or Code in Place publication.

Choose a lesson

Follow the numbered weeks to build on each idea, or open any week directly. You do not need to complete another week first.

  1. Week 1Chance

    Why would a fictional writing assistant choose its second most likely next token?

    Mark each "warm glow" result as a success. Week 2 models the count of successes across repeated runs.

  2. Week 2Models

    Can you estimate a hidden survey yes rate when each respondent reports only a total?

    Try a rate by hand. Week 3 asks which parameter values best explain the observed data.

  3. Week 3Fitting

    Can you locate a phone using the counts of packets it received from each tower?

    Fit a location to observed counts. Week 4 applies parameter fitting to a neuron's weights and bias.

  4. Week 4Single neuron

    How do two weights and a bias change a neuron's sailboat classification?

    Compute one neuron's output. In Week 5, a neuron's output becomes another neuron's input.

  5. Week 5Neural network

    Can you add pixels to a handwritten 3 so the network calls it "not a 3"?

    Attack a real tiny network in your browser. Compare three ways to choose pixels.

    Change a prediction by editing an input. Week 6 asks how to evaluate a system beyond one prediction.

  6. Week 6Breaking and auditing

    What evidence do you need before you call a crash count of three low?

    Finish with a testable audit question. Does rain increase a pedestrian detector's miss rate? Define comparable rainy and dry samples, count misses, and check whether chance could explain the difference.

How to use a lesson

Each lesson follows this sequence.

  1. A hook question gives you a concrete problem to consider.
  2. A mental model explains how the idea works.
  3. One or more live exercises compute results as you change their inputs.
  4. Collapsible math explains the equations. Open it to connect the calculations to the mental model.
  5. Checks ask you to predict a result. Make your prediction before you open the answer.
  6. Optional, collapsible deeper notes give further explanations of the week's ideas.

Connect the weeks

Each lesson opens by placing itself in the six-week arc and closes by explaining how its ideas connect to what comes next. Week 6 ends with an audit question you can test.

Use optional course notes

Most lessons include an optional, collapsible note for learners who took the course. You do not need a course login to use this package.

Open the folder offline

Keep the package folders together and open this hub in your browser without an internet connection. Every simulation, chart, and equation runs locally on your computer.
